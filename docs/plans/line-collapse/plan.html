Collapsing the Line

Five mockups for giving the conversation back the 86 vertical pixels the pipeline strip currently holds, each measured rather than estimated - and the one that was adopted, which keeps the amber alert the strip exists for.

Decided · Concept 1 adopted · mockups only, no code change

Decisions taken

Which concepts to buildConcept 1 - Condensed, the one-line ticker. Only that one.
Default density on upgradeCondensed
Does Hidden keep the attention hairlineKeep the 3px segmented hairline - held in reserve, since Hidden is not in scope

Concepts 2, 3 and 4 are kept below as the design record rather than deleted - the rejected shapes are why the adopted one won. Concept 4 is deferred, not rejected.

Collapse the bar entirely. It should be expandable to show again (pinned?). So something like pinned or unpinned, or collapsed or expanded, beyond what it already has. We want to free up more vertical real estate for the chat window.

Two vocabularies are in that sentence - pinned/unpinned and collapsed/expanded - and they are not the same axis. Separating them is most of the design work, so it is done up front rather than left to the concepts.

What the Line costs today, measured

Measured in Chromium at a 1512×900 viewport - a 14" MacBook Pro's logical size - against the real src/web/styles.css, in the console layout. These are read off the live layout, not estimated from the stylesheet:

BandUsed heightHow it is built

Two facts follow, and together they are the whole reason this is worth doing:

  • The Line is flex: none inside a height: 100dvh flex column. .app-console and .app-board do not scroll, so every pixel the strip uses is taken directly off the conversation pane. There is no scrollback that hands it back.
  • 86px of 900px is 9.6% of the window. Reclaiming all of it takes the console pane from 757px to 843px - an 11.4% taller conversation, on the surface an operator spends the whole day in.

.ls-sub - the sentence line - is 14px of content that costs 20px of band once its gap is counted, and it is the only part of the strip that is prose rather than a number. That makes it the natural first thing to give up, which is exactly what Concept 1 does.

Two axes, one control

AxisValuesWhat it governs
DensityExpanded / Condensed / HiddenHow much the strip says
PinningPinned / UnpinnedWhether the strip takes layout height or floats over the board

Crossed, that is six states, and a six-state chrome control is worse than the 86px it saves. The recommendation collapses it to three positions on one control, because pinning is only a real choice in the middle:

  • Expanded - today's two-line strip. Pinned by definition; a floating 86px panel over the board is strictly worse than the pinned one.
  • Condensed - one line, counts and tones only. Pinned.
  • Hidden - zero layout height. Unpinned by definition; the peek overlay is how you reach it.

So pinned/unpinned is not a switch the operator sets. It is implied by the density they chose. That is the honest version of the two-axis model, and it needs one control instead of two.

What must survive a collapse

The strip is not decoration, and src/web/components/LineStrip.tsx says so in its own header: it renders at a fixed height whatever it is saying, including nothing, because a strip that vanished when the fleet went quiet "would stop being the place you look". Collapsing it fights that intent directly. Everything below only works if it keeps the three things the strip exists for:

  • The amber read. tone-attention is the one signal the strip was built to carry - a session is stuck on you. A collapsed state that drops amber has not freed 86px, it has deleted the feature. Every concept keeps an amber path, and the recommended Hidden state keeps a mark on screen even with nothing else left.
  • A stable board. The strip holds its height so the cards underneath never step up and down. A collapse must be a deliberate act with a persisted result, never something that happens on its own while the operator is reading - which is why Concept 5 is written down and then rejected.
  • One gesture back. Collapsed must not mean buried.

Concept 1 - Condensed: the one-line ticker ADOPTED

Drop .ls-sub and the wires. Six inline segments - glyph, name, count - on a single row in the same 22px gutters, with the density control at the right. The single most urgent sentence is promoted to ride at the right of the row, so the one piece of prose that is ever load-bearing is the one piece that survives.

Topbar. The most conversation of any concept here, and the only one that adds no chrome to pay for it. Also the riskiest - see below.

  • frees 86px, no new chrome
  • fights the topbar rung ladder for width
  • can move --topbar-h
  • not recommended first

The topbar is already on a responsive rung ladder - fitTopbar and data-rung - that sheds controls as the window narrows, and --topbar-h is measured off that element and read by every full-height surface on the page. Adding six counts fights the ladder for width and can change the measured height. This repository's agent memory also records that adding a segment to the title bar broke guards in four separate layers. Worth doing eventually; not worth doing first, and not in the same change as the collapse.

Concept 5 - Auto-collapse on conversation focus REJECTED

Condense automatically the moment the conversation takes focus; expand on Esc. No manual state at all.

Rejected, and recorded here so it is not re-proposed: chrome that moves itself under the cursor is exactly what the strip's fixed height exists to prevent. The operator would lose the strip at the moment they clicked toward it. If it ships at all it ships as an opt-in, after the manual control exists.

  • rejected · moves chrome under the cursor

Adopted scope

Ship a two-position control - Expanded and Condensed - with Condensed as the new default. That is Concept 1 and nothing else.

  • Condensed frees 47.5px with no new gesture to learn, every drawer still reachable and amber intact per stage. Most of the available win, almost none of the risk.
  • Expanded remains reachable and unchanged, so nothing is lost - only folded.
  • Defaulting to Condensed changes what every existing operator sees on upgrade. That was asked and answered rather than assumed.

Held in reserve, with the design question already settled so it does not have to be reopened: if Hidden is ever built it keeps the 3px segmented attention hairline, and the peek overlay is the mechanism it needs in order not to be a one-way door. Concept 4 is deferred rather than rejected.

The control, as adopted

Two positions, drawn here as a segmented control for the settings panel. In the strip itself it is the single caret shown in the Concept 1 artboard. A third Hidden position can join later without renaming anything, which is the whole reason lineDensity is a named set rather than a boolean.

What the narrowed scope means for the build

Two consequences follow from the scope being Concept 1 alone, and both are cheaper than the three-state version:

  • The guards move less. test/line-strip-electron.test.ts needs one new case at 38.5px rather than a case per density, and nothing in this scope is 0px tall - so the tour's see-work:line target always exists and e2e/specs/see-work-tour.spec.ts needs no hidden-strip branch at all. That was the most awkward integration in the full proposal, and this scope avoids it outright.
  • lineDensity stays a named set anyway. Two states would fit in a boolean, and it should still not be one: the key is persisted on operators' machines, so a lineCollapsed: boolean would have to be renamed the day Hidden arrives.

How it would be built

Sketch only - enough to price the concepts, not an implementation plan.

  • State. A lineDensity: "expanded" | "condensed" | "hidden" key on UiConfig in src/shared/protocol.ts, defaulted in UI_CONFIG_DEFAULTS. That blob is the established home for exactly this kind of per-machine display preference - layout, conversationView and hiddenDisplayItems already live there, and a new key needs no migration. A named set rather than a boolean, following CONVERSATION_VIEWS, because three states are already more than a boolean and a fourth reading is plausible.
  • Render. LineStrip grows a density prop and the caret. The WorkQueue fold is the precedent to follow: one .wq-collapsed class hides the body for every branch the panel renders, so no branch can forget to fold, and the header keeps reporting the count that is still there.
  • Gesture. The caret in the strip, plus a bindable chord through the existing keybindings table.

Decisions, as answered

These were asked as selectable options in the Mission Control dashboard and resolved there:

  1. Which concepts to build - Concept 1, Condensed. Only that one.
  2. What the default density is on upgrade - Condensed.
  3. Whether Hidden keeps the attention hairline - keep the 3px segmented hairline, held in reserve, since Hidden itself is not in scope.
