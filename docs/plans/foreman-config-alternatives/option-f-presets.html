Option F · pick a posture, then open the detail

Postures, not fields - one question at rest, fourteen controls behind a disclosure

The other five options rearrange fourteen controls. This one asks whether the operator should be answering fourteen questions. Seven of them are model dropdowns that get set once and never touched, and behind all of them sits a single intent: how much rope does Foreman get. So the resting state is a choice between three named postures - Cautious, Balanced, Trusting - and the fields become the detail behind it: still all of them, still with their prose, one disclosure away, and marked wherever they disagree with the posture in force.

Click a posture card, or focus one and use the arrow keys - the summary lines and all nine prescribed fields really change. Open Show the individual settings and edit one: the heading becomes "Trusting, modified", the count under the cards is the real diff, and reset puts it back. Both measurements above are read off the laid out page after every change, so neither is a claim you have to take on trust.

Live repositories

Look at the fourth summary line on all three cards. It is identical on every one: no posture turns a completion safeguard off, because a preset that could would be a preset that quietly widens what Foreman retires without showing you - the same reason no posture touches Live repositories, and the same reason none of them touches the provider. So three of the eleven things in this panel are not on the axis the control is named for, and the honest version of this design has to say so on the screen rather than hope nobody checks. That is either the boundary of the idea or the first crack in it, depending on how many settings arrive next.

Why this one

  • It answers the question the operator actually has. Nobody opens this panel wanting to pick a Triage model; they want to decide how much Foreman may do unsupervised. Every other option makes them answer that in seven dropdowns.
  • The seven set-once dropdowns stop being questions. They are still there, still editable, still carrying their blurbs - but they are a consequence of a choice rather than the choice itself.
  • A new operator gets a defensible configuration without knowing what the cheap tier is, and can see what the safe end of the range looks like by clicking one card and reading the fields it wrote.
  • It scales the right way. An eleventh setting joins the three postures instead of adding a row to the column.

What it costs

  • It invents a new persisted concept. "Which posture am I on" is state the daemon does not store today. This mockup recovers it from the cheap tier, which works only because the three tiers happen to map one-to-one onto three cards - a fourth posture breaks that and the app needs a real column, a migration, and an answer for what happens when a config edit arrives from the settings search, a deep link, an env default, or another machine.
  • Presets encode opinions about models, and models ship constantly. Every list here is provider-scoped and dated. The day a new model lands, all three postures are subtly wrong until someone re-decides them, and an operator "on Balanced" moves without touching anything.
  • Modified states are a classic source of confusion. "Trusting, modified" is only true if the diff is computed against the RESOLVED value, env fallbacks included; get that wrong and the panel reports a posture that is not in force. And the storage question is unanswered: does a modified Trusting persist as a posture plus a patch, or as nine values that stop tracking the preset entirely?
  • Switching postures silently discards edits. Tapping a card here overwrites all nine fields, including the one you just changed. The real thing needs either a confirm or an undo, and both make the simplest screen less simple.
  • It is the largest behavioural change of the six while looking like the smallest screen. The others move controls; this one changes what the panel means. The disclosure still has to hold the entire field list at the panel's real density, so open it and compare the two measurements: expanded, this column is taller than the one the exercise set out to shorten. It is the old column plus a posture card, and it only reads as short because it starts shut.
  • Deep links land inside a collapsed region onto a field a posture may overwrite on the next tap, so foreman/skip-scout-wrapup has to open the disclosure, flash the row, and somehow say "this is preset territory".
