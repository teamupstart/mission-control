Ledger first - the column goes away and the evidence gets the width

The strongest reading of "unnecessary": the column is not merely long, it is occupying the width the ledger needs. What stays on the page is a posture bar - the readings that must never be a click away (is a worker alive, what mode, when did it last decide) and the one knob that is genuinely in-the-moment, the cheap tier. Everything set once goes behind Configure, a right-hand sheet you can still read the ledger through.

The width is the real prize. Beside the current column the Asked cell is 366px and truncates the purpose line on nearly every row; full-width it is roughly double, and most rows read whole. That column carries purpose, which is the only field that reliably differs between two rows - Needs approval: Bash covers 318 rows of a real 833-row ledger on its own. Open the sheet and watch the same measurement: 400px of it comes back, which is the honest cost and the reason the cheap tier stays out on the bar rather than going in the sheet with the rest.

Why this one

  • The page becomes the thing it is for. The ledger is the only cross-session view of Foreman's decisions anywhere in the app.
  • The Asked column roughly doubles and stops truncating the one field that distinguishes two rows.
  • Honest framing. A provider and seven model ids ARE set-once; putting them behind a button says so instead of giving them a third of the page.
  • Scales indefinitely. A sixth config card lengthens a sheet nobody is looking at, not the page.

What it costs

  • Biggest change. New sheet component, focus trap, escape handling, and a decision about what the settings-search deep links do.
  • A sheet inside a settings page is a second layer on a surface already reachable from the command palette.
  • Diverges from Inspector and Shipping, which use the same sc-split. Either they follow, or the console pattern forks.
  • The posture bar is a new judgment call - deciding what is in-the-moment enough to stay out of the sheet, forever. Get it wrong and the sheet becomes a place people open constantly, which is the worst of both layouts.
