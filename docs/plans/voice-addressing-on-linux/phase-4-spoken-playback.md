# Phase 4: Spoken playback

The [approved source](plan.md) and [implementation index](phased-plan.md) cover the mission-control repository.

## Outcome

A supervised Linux listener plays an ask or audition and the tester hears it. Capture stops before output and can resume after verified silence, including when a stop interrupts a fetch, playback or device change.

## Entry and direct dependencies

Planning publication, Linux 2, “Spoken Responses for Voice - Speech 1: The speech job and ask aloud” and “Spoken Responses for Voice - Speech 2: Mission Control voices”. All three are direct merge prerequisites. Use the operator-arranged physical microphone and speakers.

## Scope

Implement only the Linux listener side of L and physical S9 qualification. Consume the upstream speech manager, synthesizer and model. No new engine, provider, picker, browser audio or speech setting.

## Implementation and compatibility

Any implementation phase adding Linux playback has two **direct merge prerequisites**: “Spoken Responses for Voice - Speech 1: The speech job and ask aloud” and “Spoken Responses for Voice - Speech 2: Mission Control voices”. It also requires proven Linux capture stop/restart and the supervised-instance lifecycle. Contract L supersedes the addressing plan’s shell-owned speech capability and macOS-only ask path. Reuse M’s daemon-started synthesizer, on-request Supertonic 3 download (about 150 MB), Automatic F4, existing picker, and daemon-computed speech-output. Linux lists no system voices. The on-screen card is the fallback. The only Linux speech work is playback, supervision/recovery and truthful Setup reporting.

Extend the merged src/voice-listener/client.ts per its token, lease and loopback rules. Use the supervisor instance on each speech request. Integrate a cancellable device player beside sherpa.ts; keep the finite recording feeder’s semantics intact. Serialize capture closure, async audio fetch, start, drain, cancel, event reporting and reacquisition. Make repeated directives and failed requests idempotent. Reuse the daemon’s existing durable stopping state on disconnect.

**Show that the listener is silent.** Announce a supervised instance first. A live hello advertises speech.audio only when a working, qualified player is available. Before exposing a Linux job as ready, atomically stop issuing capture-enabled directives and account for every issued Linux live-input grant whose physical closure remains unproven, including expired grants, responses in flight, and standalone or retiring listeners from this or an earlier daemon run. Preserve the closure obligation before a grant becomes observable. Expiry, invalidation, a new lease, or daemon restart cannot delete it. Retire it only after the closure proof below. If recovery cannot reconstruct which grants may still own open streams, withhold playback until their closure is established. For each outstanding grant, establish that its physical input stream has finished closing and the grant cannot reopen it. Accept a generation-matched close acknowledgement after actual closure, or a verified backend guarantee that by the eligibility instant the grant has expired and the physical stream is already closed. Expiry may initiate asynchronous closure. If shutdown latency is unbounded or the guarantee cannot be recovered, keep playback in preparation until positive closure proof arrives. On daemon restart recover the same proof for prior grants. A conservative wait can cover both the maximum outstanding grant lifetime and a qualified upper bound on physical closure; an expiry timer alone supplies no device evidence. Concurrent polls cannot acquire a grant between this check and the speech hold. Give Linux input-closure preparation its own finite deadline, measured from the initial playback request and independent of retries. If proof is still incomplete at the deadline, finish the request as failed without publishing a ready job. Keep the on-screen question actionable and report the unavailable reason for an audition. Preserve outstanding closure obligations across failure, cancellation and restart. Late proof may clear an obligation for a future request but cannot play audio for the failed one. A cancelled preparation never starts playback. This preparation precedes L’s ready-to-start timer. For each holder-specific audio directive, stop and await physical capture closure, discard pending acoustic input, fetch the in-memory WAV from the existing token route, then play on the default output. Name the holder in x-mission-voice-instance on directive polls, audio fetches and event reports. Keep the existing listener lease header separately. Send started at actual playback start and ended after output drains or a stop really silences the device. Keep polling during fetch/playback. Serialize cancellation, stale callbacks, duplicate directives and event retries. Every stop silences whatever is playing, cancels pending starts and returns ended/stopped for the named job, including unknown or already-ended IDs. Output loss ends as failed after silence is certain. While playing, stopping or awaiting resolution keep local capture closed even if a directive is stale. Resume only under a fresh capture directive and an otherwise eligible mode. For every player, including an in-process player, require evidence that all affected audio-server and device queues discarded or drained the reply before releasing the capture hold. Also prove any external player terminates with the listener or account for its exit separately.

Qualify the output queue as well as the player process. A mixer, server or device can continue sounding after its client exits. Use a backend/device combination for which forced client exit demonstrably discards or drains queued audio before capture can reopen. If exit and silence are separate events, the Linux supervisor must retain its recovery gate until it also observes queue discard or drain, including after daemon restart. Neither an elapsed duration nor an empty process scope establishes device silence. Withhold playback capability on combinations that cannot supply this guarantee.

Bind each job to the qualified physical output route and observe route changes until the hold clears. A default-sink change may leave playback on the original sink or migrate queued samples. If the backend cannot pin the stream, stop on migration and retain the hold until discard or drain is proven on every affected route, including the destination. Withhold ended, exit clearance and capture while any route may still sound. A new sink must qualify before receiving a new job. A backend that cannot pin or observe the route reliably cannot advertise playback.

The silence proof must survive loss of both processes. Before any sample can enter a pinned route, persist its stable server generation and device identity against the holder in owner-only supervisor state. For a backend that permits migration, qualify it only if every potential destination is recorded durably before it can receive samples, or the audio server can reconstruct the complete set of affected routes by holder after the clients disappear. Saving a notification after migration leaves a crash window. If the server cannot give that guarantee, require a nonmigrating stream or withhold playback. Keep route evidence until all queues are proven silent and the daemon accepts finalization; distinguish server restart and ID reuse from a positively identified silent device.

## Verification and merge criteria

Run `node --test --import ./test/setup-state.mjs --import tsx test/voice-listener-protocol.test.ts test/voice-listener-lifecycle.test.ts` plus new player and upstream Speech 1/2 contract tests. Use a controllable player that withholds drain to prove capture stays closed. Exercise mismatched holders, unknown stop, duplicate directives/events, cancellation during fetch, player failure, output removal and shutdown. Repeat with the real player in a disposable Linux fixture. Give a standalone or retiring listener a valid capture directive and leave its physical input open while delaying its next poll. Request an audible reply on a different supervised listener. Assert that its speaker stays silent, including on a concurrent poll, until the old input has confirmed closure or its verified grant deadline has passed and the physical stream is closed. Exercise both paths. Request another reply just after grant expiry with the stream still closing, both in the issuing daemon and after restart. Verify the expired grant is included in preparation. Delay physical close completion beyond grant expiry and independently verify that the job stays preparing and the speakers remain silent until the input is closed. Deliver the stale response after expiry and verify no reopening or routed reply audio. Repeat the handoff with the daemon restarted and the old grant still live. Withhold all closure proof past the preparation deadline for both an ask and an audition. Verify visible fallback, a final failed request, zero playback, and retained obligations. Deliver late proof and confirm only a newly requested job can play. Conduct three open-control and three paused S9 trials: tester hears the reply, no recognized/routed self-speech with capture paused, and subsequent human speech works. If paused trials leak, test the upstream 250 ms delay; with persistent leakage withhold audio readiness and report the failed qualification. Record output device, stack and results.

**Required hardware crash trial.** On the reserved Linux desktop, start a reply long enough for the tester to hear it through the speakers. While it is still audible, abruptly terminate only the daemon and restart it against the same state home. Observe physical output independently of the listener and timestamp the microphone stream closing and reopening on the same timeline. Record the recovered holder’s stop confirmation or OS-proven exit, and any replacement listener’s first capture. Pass only if capture stays closed across restart until both the speakers are silent and the holder is cleared. If a replacement starts, its predecessor must already have exited. Verify that none of the reply is routed and that a fresh spoken message is delivered afterward. An inconclusive timeline leaves this hardware gate open.

Run the repository typecheck and lint. Run build and smoke for runtime changes, the applicable unit suite, and browser E2E for visible changes. Use Node 24 or newer and the test-state preload. Keep hardware results and screenshots in PR/workflow evidence. Record measured limitations in the technical guide. Merge when the outcome works through the stated entry point and its failure cases pass.

Run a second physical crash trial with a long audible reply and queued samples. Kill the listener abruptly mid-sound, leaving the daemon and audio server alive, and exercise the supervisor’s replacement path. Timestamp actual speaker output with an independent monitor and the replacement input opening on one timeline. Repeat with daemon recovery while output may still be queued. Require confirmed discard or drain and silence before the first capture sample; check no reply tail enters recognition and subsequent human speech succeeds. Retain evidence for each qualified output path.

During audible playback on the test desktop, switch the default sink to a second output. Observe both outputs and physical input independently. For a pinned stream verify the sound stays on its qualified sink; for a migrated stream verify that silence on both routes precedes stop confirmation and capture reopening. Include a destination without a qualified completion signal and check that capture stays closed until silence can be proven. Record the sink identities, audio-server behavior and event order.

With audio still queued after a forced migration, crash both the daemon and listener before either processes the route-change notification. Restart the daemon, recover both output identities without process memory and independently observe their last audible samples. Verify capture stays closed through proof of silence on both, then reopens and accepts new human speech. For a pinned-only backend, attempt the same switch and prove that only the recorded original route can receive samples.

## Downstream handoff and cross-phase audit

Linux 5 consumes Speech 1’s daemon-computed speech-output and reason plus actual listener audio readiness. Linux 4 consumes no Linux 3 adapter. A physical proof and an OS-backed stop are required even when waveform generation succeeded in CI.
