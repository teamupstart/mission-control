# Phase 3: Global mute and call detection

The [approved source](plan.md) and [implementation index](phased-plan.md) cover the mission-control repository.

## Outcome

The shortcut works while another application has focus. Another client using a physical microphone auto-mutes voice, and holding the chord temporarily permits bounded speech. Setup will be able to explain each unsupported capability separately.

## Entry and direct dependencies

Planning publication, Linux 2 and “Voice Addressing for Agents - Phase 11: Hotkey, call auto-mute and push-to-talk”. Recheck the merged threshold, stop semantics and time headers. Arrange X11 coverage and identify each actual Wayland compositor/portal to qualify.

## Scope

Own one desktop report producer, X11 and portal registration, and PipeWire/PulseAudio observation. Preserve browser-only presentation. Do not add a press-only global fallback, a keyboard-event logger or an audio player.

## Implementation and compatibility

Implement platform adapters beside the Linux daemon voice integration. Feed their readings into the established report schema and voice state path; use existing mute/stop actions. Source physical key and audio times on a compatible clock and verify bounds before permitting held delivery. Use Linux 2’s launch identity for supervised capture and the live lease registration for standalone capture. Document tested stacks and their limits in docs/voice.md.

Use one Linux producer of complete contract B snapshots so an idle hotkey heartbeat cannot overwrite call detection. Send transitions promptly and refresh within the 15-second report lifetime. Use the existing config, mute operation, voiceMode precedence, registeredHotkey, hotkeyError, callActive and pushToTalk. Order observation and physical key edges on the Linux CLOCK_MONOTONIC microsecond timebase. Retain the last release stamp across heartbeats. A missing release, disconnect, session lock, rebind or stale report clears a held state and discards an unprovable tail. Keep explicit mute authoritative.

For Linux push-to-talk, recognizing a phrase does not authorize delivery. Keep held segments in bounded listener memory, tagged with the capture revision. Until the daemon has verified the matching physical release, the listener withholds submission and the daemon rejects early submissions before routing. At release, discard samples outside the mapped press and release interval, decode any crossing segment again from the clipped audio, and submit only eligible words under the matching released revision. An expired hold, lost release or invalidated revision discards pending segments. Ordinary listening keeps its existing delivery behavior.

The operator chose “Retain the event-based support bar”. A timely mapped activation may start a bounded hold without a separate current-down query. When activation arrives after physical release but before deactivation, input can briefly open while another app is recording. Document this accepted limitation for each qualified portal. With both Wayland events queued after physical key-up, deliver activation, speak a complete post-release phrase, and let recognition finish while deactivation is still withheld. Observe zero routed messages before release, including for an early submission against the live held revision. Deliver the mapped deactivation and verify the queued phrase is discarded. In a separate hold, finish an in-interval phrase before release and verify it reaches the intended agent exactly once after release. Drop the release entirely and verify expiry clears pending speech without delivery. Observe any input opening, require closure on deactivation or expiry, and assert that post-release samples produce no delivered words. Reject an activation whose physical-press deadline has already elapsed. A held snapshot can outlive a lost key-up event. Track a maximum hold deadline starting at the physical press, independent of desktop heartbeats. Only fresh evidence that the chord is physically down may renew it. Where the backend cannot revalidate a physical hold, the deadline is a hard limit. On expiry, clear pushToTalk, close the input, and discard the uncertain held audio without manufacturing a release timestamp. Latch the chord inactive until an observed release and a distinct new press. Replayed presses, cached state and observer reconnects cannot rearm it.

On X11, passively grab only the configured chord in the user’s X session. Handle press/release, modifier variants, auto-repeat, layout changes and conflicting grabs. Release old registrations on disable or exit. Apply the shared tap/hold behavior: tap within 350 ms toggles mute once, a longer hold talks through call auto-mute only while held. Preserve the old chord on failed replacement where it remains registered, otherwise report no chord in force. Reflect the actual binding. When speech is active, honor the Speech 1 hotkey stop behavior.

On Wayland, exercise GlobalShortcuts CreateSession, BindShortcuts, consent, returned bindings, Activated, Deactivated, changes, revocation and session loss from the unpackaged daemon. Verify desktop identity and consent presentation without a native parent window. The portal may choose a different trigger and specifies milliseconds with an undefined base. Establish a defensible mapping of physical event time to the audio clock, including delayed delivery, or withhold the capability. Verify equivalent time conversion and wraparound for X11 event times. A callback receipt timestamp cannot establish an earlier physical release. Offer the keycap only for a current confirmed matching chord. Never use an XWayland grab as proof of global Wayland support. [Portal interface](https://flatpak.github.io/xdg-desktop-portal/docs/doc-org.freedesktop.portal.GlobalShortcuts.html)

For call detection, observe active audio capture by another client on a physical microphone. On PipeWire inspect running stream nodes and links to input sources. On PulseAudio inspect uncorked source outputs and their source/client identity. Probe each supported path, including whether a Pulse compatibility server sees native PipeWire clients. Use one authoritative observer per session with a baseline snapshot, change subscription and rescan on reconnection. Exclude only the audio streams belonging to authorized live listeners and their capture helpers, plus sinks, monitor sources, loopback sources and idle streams. Maintain lease- and grant-generation-scoped attribution for standalone and supervised listeners. Register the audio client or stream identity, including the helper actually owning it, before enabling samples. Correlate the authenticated registration with stable audio-server client/node identity and the observed owning process and start identity. For standalone launches the accepted listener lease supplies the registration authority. Supervision metadata is additional evidence for a daemon launch. Process name, executable path and shared UID alone cannot exclude another recorder. Remove attribution on stream teardown, lease expiry or server generation change; a reused ID requires new verification. If reliable attribution is unavailable, expose that limitation and keep auto-mute’s recovery rules in force. Another recorder counts even without a call. Test two simultaneous clients, the first leaving, the second leaving, and background speaker playback. Report unavailable with a reason where permissions, attribution or stack visibility prevent a reliable result. The existing toggle governs auto-mute. Dashboard mute remains available independently. [PipeWire observation](https://docs.pipewire.org/page_man_pw-dump_1.html) · [PulseAudio introspection](https://www.freedesktop.org/software/pulseaudio/doxygen/introspect_8h.html)

When auto-mute is enabled, install a capture gate before the first listener directive on daemon startup and immediately on observation expiry, observer disconnect or rescan. The daemon independently tracks the age of the last successful audio observation, even when the transport stays connected. Hotkey heartbeats carrying a cached callActive reading cannot renew it. At expiry invalidate the baseline, close capture, and start a pending rescan before ordinary mode can authorize another poll. Bound outstanding capture authorizations by this deadline. A silent observer remains pending until a fresh baseline is reconciled or auto-mute is explicitly disabled. Unknown activity during a pending baseline must not become callActive=false. Keep input closed until a complete, ordered baseline and intervening subscription events have been reconciled. An active recorder leaves capture auto-muted; a verified idle baseline permits ordinary mode evaluation. On a definitively unsupported stack, report the reason and direct the operator to disable auto-mute before using manual dashboard mute. Without activity proof, capture remains closed while auto-mute is enabled. If a previously working observer disconnects, stalls, or fails to rescan, a timeout or transient error keeps capture closed. Report the error and retry with bounded backoff. Preserve the pending baseline across retries. Capture may resume only after a reconciled observation permits it or the operator explicitly disables auto-mute, subject to other capture gates. Turning auto-mute off permits ordinary capture subject to the other gates.

When a reconciled observation changes from idle to another recorder active under auto-mute, invalidate the idle capture generation immediately and wake every affected pending directive poll with capture=false. This wakeup shares the existing mute/revision invalidation path and precedes any further renewal. A listener receiving revocation closes the physical input and discards unpermitted audio; responses issued under the older generation cannot reopen it. Its local expiry remains the backstop if revocation cannot be delivered. A subsequently authorized push-to-talk hold is a separate generation.

## Verification and merge criteria

Run `node --test --import ./test/setup-state.mjs --import tsx test/voice-state.test.ts test/voice-hotkey-shown.test.ts test/voice-utterances-http.test.ts` plus injected report/portal/X11/audio observer cases. Test timer boundaries, repeat and modifier ordering, rollback after conflict, denial/revocation, release lost during a held word, portal session replacement, and an out-of-order heartbeat. Drop the physical release after a valid press while continuing to deliver otherwise healthy desktop heartbeats beyond the hold deadline. Assert input closure at expiry, no delivery of the uncertain tail, and no reopen from repeated held snapshots. Then observe a real release and new press and verify held capture works again. Run this case for both backends, including a portal with no physical-state query. On real X11 and each claimed Wayland desktop, use a second app, speak before/during/after a hold and inspect delivered words. With auto-mute enabled and a verified idle baseline, launch the standalone listener and its selected capture helper. Observe its input stay open through multiple observer reports without a self-mute cycle. Start an unrelated recorder under the same user and verify capture closes; stop that recorder and verify one listener stream resumes. Repeat with audio-client ID reuse after reconnect. Test native and compatibility-server visibility, two recording clients, monitor streams and logout. Record which combinations earn availability and the exact reason for others.

Run the repository typecheck and lint. Run build and smoke for runtime changes, the applicable unit suite, and browser E2E for visible changes. Use Node 24 or newer and the test-state preload. Keep hardware results and screenshots in PR/workflow evidence. Record measured limitations in the technical guide. Merge when the outcome works through the stated entry point and its failure cases pass.

Start another physical recorder first, then start the daemon with auto-mute enabled and delay the observer’s initial snapshot. Inspect every directive and the OS input stream: capture stays closed before the snapshot and remains auto-muted after the active baseline arrives. Disconnect and reconnect the observer while the recorder remains active, delay its rescan, and repeat the assertions. Also leave the transport connected, keep unrelated hotkey heartbeats flowing and stop audio observations while the other recorder stays active. Advance past the observation deadline, verify input remains closed and every directive withholds capture, then force the rescan to time out and fail again. Confirm capture stays closed across both retries. Restore the observer and reconcile the still-active recorder. In a separate run explicitly disable auto-mute during the failure and verify other capture gates still apply. End the other recording and verify only a reconciled idle state permits listening. Include a second client joining during enumeration to exercise event ordering.

Also exercise expiry from an open microphone. With auto-mute on, establish a verified idle baseline and observe the listener’s physical input stream open under a capture-enabled directive. Stall only the audio observer without disconnecting it; keep directive polls, listener and hotkey heartbeats healthy. Before the deadline verify input is open. At the last audio observation’s deadline independently observe the stream close, even if a previously enabled directive arrives late. Keep the rescan pending through a retry and verify that no stream reopens. Reconcile a fresh idle baseline, withhold the next capture authorization and check the input remains closed. Deliver fresh authorization and verify that one stream opens and a new spoken phrase succeeds.

With a live microphone and auto-mute enabled, renew a healthy idle grant, leave the next directive poll pending and start another physical recorder immediately afterward. On a healthy connection, observe the active reading wake the poll with revocation and the listener input close before the old idle grant expires. Replay a delayed renewal for that generation and verify the input stays closed. Stop the recorder and verify reopening only under a fresh authorized idle baseline.
