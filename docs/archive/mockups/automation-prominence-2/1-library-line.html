01One split, two homes. The five tabs mixed build-time (workflows, Personas, actions) with run-time (runs, ensembles). The IA now states the distinction instead of blurring it: the Library answers "what have I built?", the Line answers "what is happening?".
02Runs grew hands; the Review stage shows it. A run no longer just judges - Actions let it instruct the session, and "waiting for action" is a first-class state. The drawer surfaces action waits at triage level, and ensemble handoff runs carry their ⧉ provenance.
03The overlap is resolved. Round 1's Library had a "Happening now" rail and the Line had live stages - two owners for the same information. Here the Line owns live state; the Library keeps only per-shelf "on the Line →" links, so no state renders in two places.
04Cost demoted from chrome to chip. The always-on second row becomes a purple chip (machinery tone) with the full strip's content one click away, and per-PR cost moves to the stage where PRs live. Session cards keep their per-card ≈$ facts, untouched.
05Drawers toggle, cap, and scroll; cards never scale. A stage click opens its drawer in place, pushing the board down - a second click, esc, or ✕ closes it and the board slides back up; a different stage swaps the content. The drawer is hard-capped at three rows (~38vh) and scrolls internally beyond that, so the board is always on screen - a drawer that could fill the viewport would be a fourth page, and that job belongs to "All runs →". Cards render at full size in every state.
06Cost of the concept: two navigation homes must stay honest - every Library card needs a live link and every Line object needs a "definition →" backlink, or the split becomes a wall. The per-stage fold event is the one new server contract.
