Mission Control - UI exploration - 2026-07-30

Making workflows, Personas & ensembles visible

Four approaches to the same complaint: the automation systems are buried, and nothing in the UI says what they are for. Each mockup is a full page in the app's own visual language - open them in order, they escalate from zero-chrome to full reframe.

01The problem, in facts from the codebase

One door, three systems. A single w chord + one topbar button + a 4-tab strip is the entire front door for Workflows, Personas, Ensembles and Runs. WorkflowPage.tsx · keybindings.ts
Discoverability inverts importance. Personas (now shared by two engines) and Ensembles (the expensive multi-agent feature) have zero dedicated entry points; both land on the builder tab. docs/plans/persona-versioning-and-foreman · ensemble-ux-gaps G5
Launch here, watch there. Ensembles launch from a radiogroup in the Dispatch modal header and are watched under #/workflows/ensembles - the empty state is the only pointer between them. DispatchModal.tsx · EnsembleRuns.tsx
Purpose lives in tooltips. No surface explains what a workflow, Persona or ensemble is for; ⌘K indexes only Settings; the Sitrep, backlog and command bar are blind to all three. settings-search.ts · ReportPanel.tsx

04What combines

4 composes with everything and is worth doing regardless - the palette index and the pulse segment are small and keyboard-native. 1 + 4 is the low-risk pairing: doors and findability without moving a page. 3 is the boldest single move and pairs naturally with 4 (the strip for visibility, the palette for reach). 2 subsumes 1's grouping logic, so choose one of them, not both. If the appetite is a full IA pass, 2 + 3 + 4 together is the coherent end state: Library for what you author, the line for what is happening, the palette to jump between them.
