Ensemble strategies, beyond best_of_n v1

The engine plan (docs/plans/best-of-n-swarm-dispatch/plan.md) settled the foundation: a reusable Ensemble orchestration layer above Tasks and the Dispatcher, with Best-of-N shipping as its first versioned strategy, best_of_n v1 - not as the engine's name or schema. Every member is a normal Task; a strategy is an immutable recipe composing seven policies; evaluators recommend and humans promote; artifacts are immutable Git snapshots; outcomes are not always a single winner.

This page is the strategy roadmap on top of that kernel: what each strategy is as a recipe composition, which primitive (if any) it needs beyond v1, and which are worth building next. It replaces the earlier "swarm" draft of this document, whose assumptions the engine plan materially corrected.

The through-line, updated: the engine already solved comparison validity (pinned base), completion (explicit submission), evidence honesty (reported vs observed), and safety (human-confirmed finalization, retained snapshot refs). What is left to design per strategy is exactly three things: who is launched (LaunchPolicy + information topology), how artifacts are judged (EvaluatorPolicy + stages), and what the terminal outcome is (selected / synthesized / retained / no_consensus).

Three spotlights

The three extensions this document argues for, drawn in the product's own visual language and restated on the engine's seams.

1 · Divergence mining

extends consensus / no-consensus

A consensus evaluator diffs the N artifacts' decisions, not their code. What all members agree on is filed as the summary; each divergence becomes a structured option set feeding the engine's decision stage - the same human-authority stage Best-of-N already ends in, fed by evaluator output instead of a scorecard. No new destructive authority, no new primitive beyond the consensus outcome; the swarm becomes a machine for finding the questions worth asking you.

2 · Design-off (plan ArtifactAdapter)

the adapter seam's best follow-up

Members produce design documents, not implementations - a plan ArtifactAdapter beside v1's git_snapshot. Capture, summarize, and materialize-for-evaluation are natural for a document, and the adapter's optional restore is the designed seam for "turn the winning plan into a build Task" (materializedTaskId already exists on the selected outcome). Deliberately deferred by the v1 non-goal on scout ensembles - but it is the strategy that earns big rosters, because comparison happens where candidates are cheap.

3 · Spec-first guidance

half landed in v1

Half of the earlier "rubric-first" idea is already in v1: evaluator guidance is snapshotted at ensemble creation, before any artifact exists - criteria committed before the work. The stronger half is a wave-0 spec member whose artifact (acceptance criteria) becomes the evaluator guidance for later waves (artifact-as-guidance, a small new primitive). Making those criteria executable is a separate follow-up the engine plan already names: repository-owned evaluation profiles. Until then, a reported check is labeled a claim - never rendered as observed fact.

wave 0: spec member submits criteria · wave 1: builders launch against them

Evaluator guidance · snapshotted before wave 1

3rdMaintainability and fit with repository conventions
4thScope discipline, regression surface, security risk
tieDiff size as a tie-breaker, never smallest-diff-wins

Evaluated as criteria in the guidance; executed only once evaluation profiles exist. Reported vs observed stays labeled either way.

06

The composed sequence

The endgame respects the engine plan's boundary: Ensemble is the exploration/selection stage over N sessions; the one-Session Workflow owns review, repair, and gates after promotion. Two ensemble runs and one Workflow handoff, composed at promotion boundaries - never one graph.
