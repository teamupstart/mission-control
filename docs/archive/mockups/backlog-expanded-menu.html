Backlog: five directions for the expanded menu

Today the BACKLOG stage opens the Sitrep. Each mockup below explores a purpose-built surface in its place, drawn only on state the browser already holds: the task list, the Foreman's plan order, and the blocker graph.

AThe queue drawer

Backlog joins intake, review, and decide as a fourth drawer. Plan order, one row per task, actions where your eyes already are: launch, move to top, park.

Trade-off: the most consistent answer and the least opinionated one. It reads the queue but doesn't explain it; the Sitrep link stays for the full story.

BThe autopilot planner

Answers the stage's own promise: what would autopilot take next, and why. One featured task, its reasoning spelled out, one button. The rest of the queue is a footnote.

Trade-off: superb at n=1 and builds trust in autopilot's ordering, but working the whole queue still means going somewhere else.

CThe triage strip

Capture and order, keyboard-first. File a task without leaving the bar, set priority and park inline, drag the plan order yourself. Dense rows, no prose.

Trade-off: the fastest way to work the queue and the weakest way to read it - blockers get one flag, not a story.

DThe health radar

Not a list - the shape of the queue. Age runs left to right, every task is a dot in its priority lane, and the amber zone is where work goes to rot. Blocked chains are drawn as chains, including the dead one autopilot will never unstick on its own.

Trade-off: earns its keep at thirty tasks, overkill at three. It reads the backlog's health, not its content - a dot needs a hover to become words.

EThe launchpad

Half planner, half queue. The next task gets a cockpit - agent, model, effort, branch - and the rest wait in a slim rail. For the operator who edits the dispatch before it flies.

Trade-off: the heaviest surface here; it earns the space only if you routinely override autopilot's defaults before launch.
