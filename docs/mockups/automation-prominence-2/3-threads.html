3/3Threads: every piece of work tells its own story

The reframe: automation gets no home of its own - it gets a speaking part in the story of each piece of work. A Thread is one unit of work followed end to end: where it came from, who worked it, every review round, the decision, the merge, and what it cost. The fleet gains a third layout next to Grid and Board. The Runs monitor and the ensemble dossier stop being destinations; they are chapters that appear in the thread at the moment they happened. You learn what a workflow is by watching one happen to your own task.

01Purpose through narrative. No surface has to explain that a workflow reviews and repairs - chapter 3 and 4 of your own task just showed you. The vocabulary (round, verdict, action, decision) is taught by the story using it.
02Runs and ensembles dissolve into chapters. This is the direct answer to "authoring vs work being done": Threads only shows the work being done. The run reader and the decision dossier open from a chapter, scoped to that thread - the global Runs and Ensembles tabs retire.
03The honest cost view. Per-day burn is the machine's accounting; per-thread cost is the operator's - "this fix cost $4.10" is the number you actually reason with. The topbar keeps only a small total; the real ledger lives on the work.
04Where authoring went. Workflows, Personas and Actions are edited behind a single "Catalog" entry (⌘K and one topbar item) - this concept deliberately trades authoring prominence for execution clarity, and that is its main risk.
05Cost: needs a durable thread id. Task → session → run → ensemble → PR are stitched today by four different link tables; a Thread needs one persistent work id across all of them, established at intake. UI is the easy half of this concept.
