Mission Control · defect investigation

Why Foreman double-posts
the same content

Foreman is not sending twice. One pre-PR recovery attempt writes the instruction into two independent records, and the Conversation merges both into one reading - so a single delivery is printed to the operator twice, seconds apart. The fix removes the second copy from the record that does not own it, and keeps it wherever nothing else does.

The finding

The screenshot shows one Foreman act, drawn twice at 3:36 PM: a “Foreman answered for you” card whose body is the recovery instruction, and directly beneath it a “Foreman automated turn” carrying the same instruction verbatim.

Root cause
1 line of code

recordShipRecovery put the delivered payload at the head of the note and episode brief, which the Conversation renders beside the turn that already carries it.

Injections per attempt
1

Verified in the shepherd's claim/marker machinery. A second send is refused, not merely unlikely.

Affected surfaces
3

The Conversation (chat and terminal renderings) and the Foreman drawer / decision ledger detail, which prints brief and sentText one above the other.

Paths that keep the full text
4 of 5

Only a confirmed delivery loses its copy from the brief. Escalation, ambiguous delivery, released claims and reviewer failures keep theirs.

The duplicated paragraph is the recovery instruction Foreman chose for an invited managed ship task that has gone quiet before its first pull request. It is built by structuralPayload in src/server/foreman/ship-shepherd.ts and delivered by deliverShipRecovery in src/server/foreman/worker.ts:1271.

After delivering it, the same function calls recordShipRecovery (worker.ts:1513), which wrote the audit body like this: 

const brief = [
  result.detail,                      // the whole instruction, again
  decisionSummary && decisionSummary !== result.detail
    ? `Completion decision: ${decisionSummary}`
    : null,
  `Quiet age: ${quietMinutes} minutes. Delivery: ${result.delivery}. Next: ${next}.`,
].filter(Boolean).join("\n\n");

result.detail on the delivered path is the payload that was just injected. That string is then written to three places for one act: the session note, the episode brief, and the episode sentText - while a fourth copy, the turn itself, is already in the transcript.

How one delivery becomes two posts

The two records never meet on the server. They are joined at render time, by mergeConversation, which is exactly where the repetition becomes visible.

Why it is not a double send

Worth stating explicitly, because it is the reading that would have led to the wrong fix. The delivery path is already hardened against spending an attempt twice, and every guard below was read rather than assumed.

GuardWhereWhat it refuses
Compare-and-set claim on an exact marker routes.ts:5500, claimPromptedRecovery The daemon re-derives the decision and refuses a claim whose reason, attempt, marker, episode key or decision generation does not match. Two workers cannot both claim one attempt.
Post-claim freshness re-check worker.ts:1305-1355 Twenty conditions are re-read immediately before the inject. Any drift releases the claim positively and records “confirmed undelivered” rather than typing.
Ambiguity is spent, not retried ship-shepherd.ts, decideShipRecovery lastDelivery === "confirmed_undelivered" retries the same attempt; every other outcome, including unknown, advances the attempt. A lost response cannot become a second send of the same text.
Episode upsert on (note_key, marker) db.ts:4484 Re-recording one attempt updates its row instead of adding a second, so a retried same-marker attempt cannot produce two cards.
Immediate-held route is claimed too worker.ts:2322-2350 The held-completion fast path and the quiet-window shepherd spend attempts through the one deliverShipRecovery helper, under the same claim.

What remains true after three attempts

Attempts 2 and 3 do re-send a byte-identical instruction, 40 and 80 minutes later. That is the designed retry budget, not the defect reported - the screenshot's two copies are 0 seconds apart and both belong to attempt 1/3.

The other surface it shows on

The same subtraction was owed in a second place. ForemanEpisodeCard renders with detail in the Foreman drawer and in the Settings decision ledger, where the body and the Resolution block appear one above the other:

  • brief → <div class="fe-brief markdown"> - carried the instruction.
  • sentText → <p class="fe-res-text"> under “Resolution” - carries the same instruction.

That card already suppresses one duplicate for the same reason (“a menu send types nothing, so sentText IS the label there, and printing both reads as two separate things having happened”). The recovery path simply was not covered by it.

Cases specifically checked and deliberately left alone

  • Attempts 2 and 3. Identical payload, new marker, new card, 40/80 minutes apart. Intended retry behaviour; suppressing it would silently shorten the recovery budget.
  • Daemon restart between the inject and the render. src/server/injections.ts is deliberately in-memory, so after a restart the turn loses its origin and renders as the operator's own command line. The instruction is still on screen once - the attribution is a separate, pre-existing gap and was not changed here.
  • The worker reads a note brief back. It does, in exactly one place: restoredShipRecoveryReviewFailure parses the first line of the review-failure note (worker.ts:1410). That note is written by a different function, which this change does not touch.
  • Workflow Persona context. readLiveWorkflowIntent reads episode.brief as a human decision's rationale, but filters to resolvedBy === "you". A Foreman-resolved recovery episode never reaches it.
  • Pipeline triage. pipeline-triage.ts also passes episode.brief onward, but only for situation: "pipeline" episodes. Recovery episodes are situation: "ship-recovery".
  • Transcript paging. Episodes are fetched for the whole session while the transcript pages backwards, so at the very top of a loaded window a card can briefly appear without its turn. That is an argument for keeping the record readable, which the surviving purpose and audit lines do - not for keeping the whole paragraph.

The fix

Move the audit body into a pure function beside the policy that produces it, and drop the instruction from it only when the episode itself proves the words survive elsewhere. 

export function shipRecoveryBrief(input: ShipRecoveryBriefInput): string {
  const detail = input.detail.trim();
  const summary = input.decisionSummary?.trim() || null;
  const recordedElsewhere = input.sentText !== null && input.sentText.trim() === detail;
  const quiet = Math.max(0, Math.floor(input.quietMinutes));
  return [
    recordedElsewhere ? null : detail,
    summary && summary !== detail ? `Completion decision: ${summary}` : null,
    `Quiet age: ${quiet} minutes. Delivery: ${input.delivery}. Next: ${input.next}.`,
  ].filter((line): line is string => Boolean(line)).join("\n\n");
}

Three properties are doing the work:

  1. It reads sentText, never the delivery word. Only sentText is evidence that the words are recorded somewhere else. Every non-delivery records none, so those bodies stay whole automatically rather than by a branch someone has to remember to add.
  2. It compares the strings. If a future caller sends something other than detail - the bounded ambiguous-diff reviewer writes its own instruction - both halves are kept, because neither is a copy of the other.
  3. The audit line is unconditional. The body can never be empty, and the card still reports which attempt this was, how long the session had been quiet, what became of the send, and when the next attempt is due.

It lives in ship-shepherd.ts rather than in worker.ts for the reason episodeFromPlan lives in verdict.ts: worker.ts calls main() at import, so a rule written inline there can never be unit-tested - and this one decides whether an operator is shown the same instruction twice.

Why it breaks nothing

No information is lost on any path

On the one path where the brief gives up the instruction, the episode's sentText still holds it verbatim and the drawer and ledger print it under “Resolution”. In the Conversation the turn itself is the copy. Every other path keeps its full body.

  • No behaviour change to delivery. Nothing in the claim, marker, attempt, schedule, escalation or release logic is touched. The only change is what text is written into an audit body after the act.
  • No note reader is affected. The one server-side reader of a note brief parses a different note, written by a different function. ForemanStrip never rendered the brief at all; ForemanRecommendation reads it only for pane-dialog and review notes.
  • The decision summary rule is unchanged. A summary that is the detail is still printed once or not at all, so escalations do not gain a repeated line.
  • The existing suites still pass unchanged. 11,507 unit tests, including every Foreman, episode and conversation file, with no assertion edited to accommodate this.

That distinction matters: the fake agent echoes each turn back as “Mock reply to: …”, and counting the transcript would have counted the child quoting its instruction - which is the agent reading, not Foreman repeating.

What this did not establish

  • Whether this is the only shape of the complaint. The reported screenshot is a pre-PR ship recovery, and that is the only Foreman writer that put a delivered payload into an audit body - the work-queue, wrap-up and PR-follow-up injectors write no note at all, and the ordinary pane-dialog path writes reasoning rather than the answer. If Foreman is seen repeating itself in some other situation, that is a different report.
  • Live reproduction with a real model. Every run here used the fake agent fixtures; no tokens were spent. The recovery was replayed through the same routes the worker calls, not by driving the worker's own reviewer.
  • Historical rows. Episodes already written keep their duplicated briefs. Nothing backfills them, and nothing needed to - the duplicate is cosmetic and the rows remain accurate.
  • The attribution gap after a restart. A Foreman turn loses its author label when the daemon restarts, because injections.ts is intentionally unpersisted. It was read and ruled out as a cause here; it was not investigated as a defect of its own.
