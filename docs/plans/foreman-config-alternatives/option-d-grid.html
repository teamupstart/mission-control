Card grid - turn the page 90 degrees and the column stops existing

The other options shorten the left column or hide it. D deletes the idea of a column. The ledger goes full width at the top, because it is the reason anyone opens this panel, and the six configuration cards fall underneath it into repeat(auto-fit, minmax(300px, 1fr)). Nothing is collapsed, nothing is behind a tab, and nothing takes a second click: every control that is on the panel today is on the screen at once. The page simply reads top to bottom - the evidence, then the settings that produced it.

Nothing here got shorter. It got wider. The same fourteen controls and the same prose are on the page; the grid spends horizontal space instead of vertical, and it only has that space because this section is full width. So the height is a function of your window rather than of the design. Measured in Comfortable, this block is 810px at a 1440px window, 1052px at 980px, 1185px at 900px, and 1659px once auto-fit drops to a single column below about 640px - at which point D is today's panel with the ledger moved above it rather than beside it. Drag the window and watch the chip. It is the one option in this set that no single number can carry.

Why this one

  • Nothing is hidden. No tab, no accordion, no sheet. Every control the panel has is visible and one click from edited, which no other option manages.
  • The ledger gets the full width. The Asked column stops being cut in half by a form nobody reads twice, and that column is the part with the sentences in it.
  • Reading order matches the reason you opened the panel. Evidence first, settings second. Today it is the other way round for no stated reason.
  • Cheap and reversible. No control, no copy and no data-anchor changes; the diff is which container the cards sit in.

What it costs

  • You can no longer see a decision and the setting that made it at the same time. Side by side, they were both on screen; stacked, you scroll past one to reach the other. That is a real loss and it is the whole point of the split layout D throws away.
  • It keeps the most total pixels of any of the six. A, B, C, E and F all remove or defer something. D removes nothing - it re-flows it. Measured as area, this is the largest panel on the table.
  • Ragged bottom edge. Models is roughly four times the height of Posture, so every row is as tall as Models and the short cards sit over a band of nothing - visible in both densities above. Stretching them only moves the emptiness inside the card; equalising the content is a rewrite of the copy, not a layout fix; and the thing that would actually fix it, masonry, is still not shippable.
  • It forks the console pattern. Inspector and Shipping are the same sc-split two-column layout. Either they follow Foreman into a grid, which is three panels of work nobody asked for, or the three consoles stop looking like each other.
  • The layout moves under you. Card positions depend on window width, so a deep link lands somewhere different on a laptop than on a monitor and there is no stable place to remember Safeguards being.
